# Addendum — Post-Spot-Check Review (2026-04-21)

**Context:** Since the initial assessment (2026-04-20), new spot-check experiments have been run to investigate the `auto_scale`, gauge-averaging, and β_eff cross-check concerns. This addendum reviews those results and updates the recommendations.

## 1. auto_scale — Much Worse Than Initially Assessed

### Embedded graphs (the main paper's geometry)

You ran a paired design on **embedded** random-3-regular problems (`FixedEmbeddingComposite`, `chain_strength=1.0`) with `auto_scale=True` vs `False` back-to-back. The results are devastating for the legacy Phase 3/4 lambda sweep:

| Condition | auto_scale=True | auto_scale=False | ΔM |
|-----------|-----------------|------------------|-----|
| λ=0.15, W=0, E=20 | 0.9945 | 0.040 | **+0.954** |
| λ=0.20, W=0, E=20 (rep 1) | 0.987 | 0.0075 | **+0.979** |
| λ=0.20, W=0, E=20 (rep 2) | 0.988 | 0.0030 | **+0.985** |
| λ=0.20, W=0, E=20 (rep 3) | 0.990 | 0.0030 | **+0.987** |
| λ=0.20, W=0, E=20 (rep 4) | 0.9835 | 0.0045 | **+0.979** |
| λ=0.25, W=0, E=20 | — | — | ~+0.95 (from `spot_check_embedded_repeat`) |
| λ=0.30, W=0, E=20 | 0.0375 | 0.0 | +0.038 |
| λ=0.50, W=0, E=20 | 0.0 | 0.0 | 0.0 |

**Interpretation:** For embedded problems near the lambda crossover, `auto_scale=True` completely **freezes** the subsystem (M ≈ 1.0), while `auto_scale=False` shows the expected relaxation (M ≈ 0.0). The effect is:
- **Reproducible:** 4 independent repeats at λ=0.2 all show ΔM ≈ 0.98.
- **Not shot noise:** The difference is 50–100× larger than the bootstrap uncertainty on M.
- **Concentrated at weak coupling:** At λ ≥ 0.3 the effect vanishes; at λ = 0.5 both settings agree.

**Impact on the paper:** The main-text lambda sweep (Fig. 2b) was submitted with `auto_scale=True` (SDK default) and `EmbeddingComposite` with `uniform_torque_compensation` chain strength. The spot check shows that with `chain_strength=1.0` the artifact is already catastrophic. If `uniform_torque_compensation` produced a higher chain strength, the artifact could be even stronger.

**The crossover location reported in the paper (λ_c ≈ 0.2 on Advantage2) is likely an artifact of auto_scale rescaling the embedded Hamiltonian.** The true crossover for the nominal (h, J) appears to sit at λ < 0.2, possibly as low as λ ≈ 0.15.

### Native graphs (no embedding)

A separate native-graph spot check (`spot_check_native_transition`) shows the opposite directional trend at strong coupling:

| Condition | auto_scale=True | auto_scale=False | ΔM |
|-----------|-----------------|------------------|-----|
| λ=0.50, W=0, E=20 (native) | 0.0005 | 0.9425 | **−0.942** |
| λ=0.30, W=0, E=20 (native) | 0.9985 | 0.706 | +0.293 |
| λ=0.25, W=0, E=20 (native) | 1.0 | 0.931 | +0.069 |

At λ=0.5, native graphs with `auto_scale=False` show **complete memory retention** (M = 0.94), while `auto_scale=True` gives **relaxation** (M ≈ 0). This is the opposite of the embedded case.

**Why the reversal?** The mechanism is unclear without instrumenting D-Wave's internal rescaling, but the empirical pattern is robust:
- **Embedded + weak λ:** auto_scale=True inflates memory (freezes the system).
- **Native + strong λ:** auto_scale=True suppresses memory (relaxes the system).

Both effects are large enough to shift crossover locations by Δλ ≈ 0.1–0.2.

### What you must do

1. **Do not trust any Phase 3/4 lambda-dependent result submitted with `auto_scale=True`.** The lambda sweep (Fig. 2b) and the 2D (λ, W) crossover grid (Fig. 3b) need to be regenerated.
2. **Re-run the lambda sweep with `auto_scale=False`** on both QPUs, using the same embedding protocol as the main paper (or, preferably, native graphs to eliminate embedding entirely).
3. **Verify that the environment-size sweep (Fig. 2a) is safe.** It was run at λ=0.5, where the spot checks show agreement between auto_scale settings for both native and embedded geometries. It is likely valid, but spot-check one or two |E| points with `auto_scale=False` to confirm.
4. **Verify the disorder sweep (Fig. 3a) is safe.** It was run at λ=0.5, same reasoning as above.
5. **Add a dedicated Methods paragraph** explaining the auto_scale issue, the spot-check verification, and the explicit `auto_scale=False` setting used for all reproduced figures.

## 3. MPL β_eff Cross-Check — Inappropriate Diagnostic

You ran maximum-pseudolikelihood (MPL) temperature estimation on the Phase 3C disorder-sweep samples. The results show massive disagreement with the single-qubit probe:

- **Advantage2:** β_probe = 7.219, β_MPL median ≈ 0.3–1.8 (ratio 0.04–0.25)
- **System 6.4:** β_probe = 4.289, β_MPL median ≈ 0.5–2.7 (ratio 0.08–0.62)

**This disagreement does NOT mean the probe calibration is wrong.** MPL assumes the samples are drawn from a **global Gibbs distribution** P(σ) ∝ exp(−β H(σ)). Your experiment explicitly violates this assumption:
- The environment is fixed in an ordered product state, not thermal.
- The subsystem marginal is a **conditional** thermal state, not an unconditional one.
- In the memory-retaining regime (M > 0.05), the distribution is explicitly initial-state dependent and non-thermal.

MPL is therefore measuring the effective temperature of a **model that does not describe the data**. The disagreement is expected physics, not a calibration failure.

**Recommendation:** Do **not** report MPL as a β_eff cross-check in the paper. It will confuse reviewers. The single-qubit probe is the correct estimator for your protocol because it measures the Boltzmann slope produced by the **same reverse-anneal schedule** on a trivial Hamiltonian where conditional vs unconditional is irrelevant.

If you want a genuine cross-check, use the single-qubit probe on **two different bias values** (e.g., h = 0.25 and h = 0.5) and verify that both give the same β_eff within stderr. This tests internal consistency of the probe protocol without invoking an incorrect global-thermal model.

## 5. Strategic Note on the Native-Graph Pivot

Your native-graph spot check reveals that at λ=0.5, native Zephyr subgraphs **do not relax** with `auto_scale=False` (M = 0.94 at |E|=20). The paper's main environment-size sweep uses embedded random-3-regular graphs, which relax at |E|≥8, λ=0.5.

This suggests a **connectivity effect**: native graphs have higher degree and more boundary edges, but they also have different local topology than random 3-regular. The fact that native graphs are frozen at |E|=20, λ=0.5 while embedded graphs relax implies the embedding itself (or the chain dynamics) is participating in the relaxation mechanism.

This is actually an **interesting physics result**, but it complicates the claim that the relaxation is purely a property of the nominal Hamiltonian. You should:
- Acknowledge the native/embedded difference in the supplementary.
- Consider adding a native-graph |E| sweep with `auto_scale=False` to establish whether native graphs relax at larger |E|.
- Be careful not to overclaim that the effect is "universal across graph geometries" until native and embedded are compared at the same scale.
