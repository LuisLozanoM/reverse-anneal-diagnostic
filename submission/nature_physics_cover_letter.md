Dear Editors,

Please consider our Article, "Subsystem relaxation in a programmable quantum annealer", for publication in Nature Physics.

The manuscript reports a controlled subsystem-environment experiment on two programmable D-Wave quantum annealers. A six-qubit subsystem is coupled to an on-chip environment during reverse annealing, while the bath size, subsystem-environment coupling, disorder strength, environment preparation, pause point, graph geometry and QPU generation are varied. The central result is that the subsystem becomes initial-state independent when the environment is sufficiently large or strongly coupled, while quenched disorder and atypical environment preparations arrest this relaxation. In the relaxed regime, the final subsystem readout is accurately described by a calibrated conditional Boltzmann marginal across graph geometries, coupling strengths, disorder levels and two QPU generations.

We believe the work is appropriate for the diverse readership of Nature Physics because it connects statistical mechanics, quantum simulation, open-system dynamics and quantum annealing hardware. It addresses a concrete open question in the quantum annealing literature: whether a sufficiently large annealer can act as an effective bath for its own subsystems. The experiments also separate two issues that are often conflated in annealer studies: the form of the relaxed readout and the dynamical route by which that readout is reached. In a mixed-frustration regime, matched-temperature single-spin-flip Glauber dynamics fails to reproduce the observed relaxation rate, consistent with a transverse-field-assisted pathway during reverse annealing.

The manuscript is deliberately framed within the limits of the experiment. It does not claim isolated-system ETH, universal many-body thermalization, or quantum computational advantage. D-Wave QPUs are open quantum systems, and the reported relaxation occurs during a reverse-anneal protocol involving transverse-field dynamics, device noise, cryogenic coupling and readout. These caveats are stated in the Introduction, Discussion and Methods.
